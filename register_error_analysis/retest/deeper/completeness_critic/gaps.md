# Completeness critic: what is still missing or unverified

After the synthesis, a separate agent read `../README.md` and the lens directories and listed open gaps. Quick checks it ran are in this directory (`c01_quick_checks.py` and its CSVs).

## 1. About 70 of the remaining first-occurrence units have never been confirmed by a test that does not use SAbR: modes B (25 units), C (20), F (11), D0 (9) and 'other' (5). Only D/E (80 units, by FR4 superposition) and mode A (48/57) were checked independently. Controls were never scanned for slips the detector missed.

**Why it matters.** Every 'link' depends on the error set being real, and the gap-placement artifacts found so far all sit in exactly these modes: 16 units in B/C/F, the 'same FR3 error across labs' pattern, and the capsid and bovine clusters. Several indel patterns are still unconfirmed: FR3 gaps at 85, 89 and 94, 66–68, 74–75, and κ CDR2 59–62. Species effects (rabbit OR 3.3, bovine) may partly come from sparse ANARCI germline HMMs. The canonical-pull result is a SAbR-vs-ANARCI length difference, which is the very quantity a gap convention distorts. Its 'easier to detect' alternative was never tested. Counts: completeness_critic/ledger_units_by_mode_flag.csv.

**Suggested analysis.** (a) Extend the slot test in case_files_DE/scripts/s02_struct.py to every run. Use each antibody's own ImmuneBuilder/IgFold model (scratchpad/pred/full/{immunebuilder,igfold}) as the reference: superpose on framework Cα with the run ±3 excluded, then assign each deposited residue to the nearest predicted Cα slot. A run counts as confirmed when its residues sit |delta| slots off in the SAbR direction. Get the null rate from the 165 control predictions in struct_unusual (s07_ib_run). (b) Run the same test on about 1,000 random 'clean' EM chains, predicted with ImmuneBuilder, to estimate missed slips and their direction. (c) Re-run the high-resolution gap check (case_files_ABCF/s09_hires_gap_check.py) for the unconfirmed indel patterns on X-ray structures at ≤2.5 Å. (d) Re-test canonical pull, rabbit/bovine/alpaca, H2-9 and κ L1-17 on the confirmed set only.

## 2. The headline associations were never re-run on one cleaned error set. struct_unusual, literature_dean and seq_novelty still include the amyloid fibrils. No lens removed the 16 gap-artifact units, the 5 chain-end units or the near-copies. The synthesis rechecked only canonical pull and three CDR-length tests. Eight verify directories have no notes.md, so the README's verdicts for them cannot be audited: no_competitive_pressure ×2, ARTIFACT_INDEL_integrity, PM1_less_competition, amyloid_integrity, cis_pro_kappa8_integrity, new_team_integrity and partner_chain. The README also says '48 checks', but there are 37 verify directories.

**Why it matters.** 17–26% of EM units are artifacts or copies, and they cluster by lab, antigen class (capsid), species (bovine, rabbit), mode (C, F, D+) and both-chain co-occurrence (9DN2, 8JAP). They can therefore inflate or create the new-team, capsid/enzyme, VH→VL, citation, Krios and non-human-germline signals.

**Suggested analysis.** Build three outcome definitions from synthesis/artifact_ledger_runs.csv: strict, loose, and 'confirmed' (from gap 1). Re-run each headline test at its original unit using the verifiers' frames (e.g. verify/*/frame.csv.gz, entries_frame.csv.gz, units_frame.csv.gz): κ Pro8 (unit level, also excluding runs within 10 Å of the IMGT 6–12 kink), beyond-site Ramachandran, V-minus-non-V gap, framework RMSD, VH→VL co-occurrence, new team (2022–23), capsid/enzyme, citations, authors per antibody, Krios, non-human germline, H2-9 and κ L1-17. Report all three versions side by side. Write the missing verifier notes from their logs and CSVs, or drop those verdicts from the README.

## 3. The 'modelling fingerprint' (all-trans κ Pro8) does not follow the error trend, and its sufficiency was never tested. My quick check (EM κ units with Pro8, error vs clean; completeness_critic/kappa_pro8_by_period_units.csv) gives these numbers. All-trans share of units: 30% (≤2019), 36% (2020–21), 34% (2022–23), 44% (≥2024). Error rate in all-trans units: 12.9%, 12.2%, 7.0%, 3.5%. Error rate in cis units: 0–0.8% in every period. The verifier also showed that 34 of 38 κ error units have a run within 10 Å of the Pro8 kink, and only 4 units are truly distant (1 of them cis). The 'whole-model' reading of Pro8 therefore rests on very few units.

**Why it matters.** The synthesis presents the fingerprint as the common thread. But it is increasingly common in clean models: it is a near-necessary marker with a falling positive predictive value, not the link. The drop in errors after 2021 happened within the all-trans route. Something else separates, among all-trans models, the ones that slipped, and that has not been looked for.

**Suggested analysis.** (a) Model the fingerprint itself as the outcome across all ~3,000 EM κ chains and ~1,270 units: year, resolution, irm, fit, AlphaFold start, recorded ISOLDE/Coot/Rosetta/Phenix, lab experience, SARS-CoV-2. ISOLDE entries are 32% cis vs 62% (n=74, ≥2022). sw_ModelAngelo and sw_Namdinator are 0 for all 15,865 chains, so model-building tools are simply not in the metadata (see gap 5). (b) Within all-trans κ units only (n≈485; 33 errors), compare error with clean on unbuilt neighbours, local resolution, atypical lengths, new team, starting model, beyond-site Ramachandran and period. (c) For entries whose pdbx_initial_refinement_model (modelling.jsonl) names a PDB template, check whether the template has cis Pro8 and whether the deposited cis status follows it (inherited vs rebuilt). (d) Define a VH and λ equivalent, such as the antibody-vs-rest Ramachandran gap, and repeat (a) and (b).

## 4. The most striking pattern, the 2020–21 peak and the later fall, is still unexplained and was never re-estimated with clustering by paper. Unit-level EM error rate (completeness_critic/em_error_rate_by_half_year.csv): 8.6% in 2020H2 and 7.1% in 2021H1, against 2.5–4% afterwards. But there is a second bump of 7.2% in 2023H2, where 9 of 23 error units come from one capsid paper (Zhu P.). In 2020H2, 9 of 16 units come from two labs (Zhou Q., Lou Z.). Only 2021H1 is spread out: 23 units from 18 labs.

**Why it matters.** The new-team effect is confined to 2022–23 and does not explain the peak, and the fingerprint does not track it (gap 3). Competition, software and people are null. So the time trend is the main unexplained structure in the data, and part of the 'peak' may be two or three papers.

**Suggested analysis.** (a) Fit a GEE or logit with a time spline, clustered by paper, plus leave-top-k-papers-out and one-unit-per-paper versions, to establish which half-years are elevated after clustering. (b) Split 2020–21 vs 2022–25 into a composition part and a rate part (Oaxaca–Blinder or direct standardisation) over resolution, SARS-CoV-2 share, irm (AlphaFold starts were 0% before 2023 and 3–7% after), fit, local refinement, new team, fingerprint, entry Q and Fv-only models. (c) Interrupted time series at candidate breaks: AF2/ColabFold (Jul–Aug 2021), ABodyBuilder2/ImmuneBuilder (2022–23), ModelAngelo (2023), wwPDB half-map mandate and validation-report changes. Compare the fits by AIC. (d) Survivorship check: scan obsoleted or superseded antibody EM entries (files.rcsb.org/pub/pdb/data/status/obsolete.dat and the obsolete coordinate files) with SAbR/ANARCI, to see whether errors were later corrected and whether the correction rate changed over time.

## 5. The workflow interpretation (traced or threaded without antibody priors, loose refinement) was never tested on all error papers. Methods text was coded only for the case files: about 40 error papers (29 ABCF and 27 DE cases, 48–59% with open-access text). PDB metadata cannot answer it: 65 of 70 κ error chains record no starting model, and ModelAngelo, Namdinator and homology tools are essentially never recorded.

**Why it matters.** Several lenses point to the paper's modelling route as the link, but the route itself was observed for only a minority of papers. The case-file comparisons (AlphaFold 7% vs 24%, rigid body 7% vs 27%) were n.s. at that n.

**Suggested analysis.** Code the methods of all ~110 published error papers and ~300 clean papers matched on year, antigen class and resolution. Sources: Europe PMC OA full text, plus bioRxiv/medRxiv JATS via api.biorxiv.org/details (jatsxml link) for the ~30% with preprints, which raises coverage. Fields: starting-model source (named PDB ID and its CDR lengths and cis-Pro8 status; SWISS-MODEL, Modeller, ABodyBuilder 1/2, PIGS, RosettaAntibody, AF2/ColabFold, IgFold, ModelAngelo, de novo in Coot); 'mutated to the sequence'; fitting (Chimera fit-in-map, rigid body, Namdinator, MDFF); refinement (Phenix RSR with or without Ramachandran/SS restraints, ISOLDE, Rosetta, number of cycles); Fv-only vs Fab. Test the route against error, the κ fingerprint and the Ramachandran gap, with clustering by paper. As the README suggests, contact a sample of depositors of flagged entries.

## 6. Register errors in the antigen chains of the same entries were never measured. The claim that the problem is 'antibody-specific, not entry-wide' rests only on antigen Ramachandran and clash statistics. Those are normal, but clashes are entry-wide.

**Why it matters.** This is the cleanest test between two explanations: (a) a generic map-interpretation or time-pressure problem, which predicts antigen slips too, and (b) an antibody-template or threading workflow, which predicts slips only in the V domains. A positive result would also link antibody errors to the general 3–5 Å register-error literature (conkit-validate reports 1 in 6 entries).

**Suggested analysis.** Map antigen chains to UniProt with pdb_chain_uniprot.tsv.gz (SIFTS). For well-covered antigens (SARS-CoV-2 RBD/NTD/S2, HIV Env gp120/gp41, influenza HA, the common capsid proteins), superpose each EM antigen chain on a ≤2.5 Å X-ray reference or the AlphaFold DB model, in local 30-residue windows. Assign residues to reference Cα slots and flag runs of ≥3 shifted residues. Compare the rate per 1,000 antigen residues between error and clean entries, within antigen family and period, clustered by paper. If available, cross-check against the published conkit-validate/checkMySequence PDB-wide predictions.

## 7. No error was checked against the experimental map. The evidence on density is contradictory and partly unverified. One finding puts 48% of errors next to poor density vs 16% of controls. Against that, the map-intrinsic local resolution at error sites sits at the 48th percentile (134 sites, only about 39% of entries have half maps; not adversarially verified). That analysis concluded that the low Q-score at error sites is a consequence of the error.

**Why it matters.** Whether the map's side-chain density could tell the registers apart decides between 'avoidable, careless or rushed building' and 'uninterpretable density'. That distinction is central to the user's pressure hypothesis. It is also the most direct confirmation that each flagged run is a real error. The check is feasible: https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/emdb/structures/EMD-xxxx/map/ downloads work, and gemmi installs with pip.

**Suggested analysis.** For each confirmed error with a map (start with about 60 EM units across modes), build the corrected-register model by relabelling the run's sequence by −delta on the deposited backbone, or fit the ImmuneBuilder model onto the framework. Compute side-chain Q-score and atom inclusion over the run for the deposited and corrected registers. Do the same for ±1 relabelled windows in matched clean chains as a negative control. Report the fraction of errors where the map clearly favours the true register. Recompute local resolution at the site with half maps where available, and verify the pdb_meta local-resolution percentile result.

## 8. Same-antibody natural experiments were barely used. The same V sequence solved more than once, wrong in one deposition and right in another, holds the antibody, germline and lengths fixed. Only 5 within-paper D/E groups were examined (inconclusive), plus anecdotes (12TY two CDR3 variants; 7CZR identical CDR3s for two clones; 6XJA poly-Ala). Whether the deposited sequence itself is wrong was never checked systematically.

**Why it matters.** About 13% of error units have an earlier same-antibody structure, and many have later ones. Comparing within an antibody removes every antibody-level confounder and directly tests map quality, team, starting model and period. A 'wrong SEQRES, right model' class, which is a provenance error rather than a register error, would be a distinct link and would also contaminate the error set.

**Suggested analysis.** Group chains by seqkey/vseq, or ≥98% V identity with an identical CDR3 (chains.csv). Find antibodies with both an error copy and a correct copy, within and across papers, and compare resolution, local Q and resolution at the site, starting model, team experience, the κ fingerprint and deposition order using conditional logit. For sequence provenance, compare the deposited V sequences of error units and matched clean units with independent sources: CoV-AbDab, GenBank or patent sequences via NCBI E-utilities, and other PDB entries of the same named clone. Count CDR3 or framework indel discrepancies.

## 9. Competitive pressure, the user's hypothesis, was measured only with PDB deposition dates and UniProt-accession or 30%-family matching. Missing: timing measured on preprints or submission, 'scooped during review' measures, epitope-level crowding within SARS-CoV-2, and a formal statement of what effect size the data rule out.

**Why it matters.** Deposition dates lag submission and are often set by journals, and accession matching produced the spurious 'fewer competitors' result. The verifier says the data cannot exclude modest harm (up to about 3×) at SARS-CoV-2 levels of crowding. That leaves the user's main hypothesis open rather than refuted.

**Suggested analysis.** Use paper_meta's received and accepted dates (91% coverage) and preprint dates (28%; bioRxiv API). Count, per paper: (a) same-antigen-family papers from other labs that were published or preprinted between this paper's submission and acceptance, or within 30/90 days before submission; (b) whether a competitor's first antibody structure of the same epitope appeared before this paper's submission. Classify SARS-CoV-2 epitopes (RBD classes 1–4, NTD, S2) from the antigen interface residues already in antigen_competition (assembly-1 interfaces) or from CoV-AbDab labels, and test crowding at the epitope level within SARS-CoV-2. Give equivalence bounds (TOST, or the upper 95% CI per doubling) for each measure.

## 10. Small-n and post-hoc leads have no held-out replication, and external Deane/Dunbrack resources were not used. The leads: VUB/VIB nanobody source (9/31), mouse IGKV8-30 (4/41), κ cis-Pro95 (6/26, unverified), H2-9 OR 2.45 and κ L1-17 (unverified), the canonical-CDR deviation 0.76 vs 0.89 (ImmuneBuilder-based, unverified), the sheet deficit, complex size, IGHV4-34 (5/31), and new team being significant only in 2022–23. Multiple testing used BH within each lens only. A naive pooled BH over about 1,194 lens tests (my quick pooling) still leaves the post-hoc VUB lead at q=0.014, because definitional tests with tiny p-values drag the threshold. Pooled BH is therefore no substitute for replication.

**Why it matters.** Several of these are now written as 'mildly atypical' antibody features in README §7, and could be read as links. literature_dean built its own PDB/ANARCI reference rather than using SAbDab, PyIgClassify2 or OAS. So the 'Deane-group norms' comparison the user asked for is an approximation.

**Suggested analysis.** (a) Replication set: cryo-EM antibody entries released after the scan freeze, and/or extend the SAbR/ANARCI scan to EM antibody entries at 2.5–3.0 Å and >5 Å (antigen_competition's PDB-wide list of 11,972 antibody entries). Pre-register the leads above and test them once. (b) Replace the ImmuneBuilder-based canonical metric with PyIgClassify2 per-chain cluster labels, including 'none' or outlier assignments. Use the SAbDab summary and CDR clusters for the length and orientation references, and OAS positional frequencies for residue and germline rarity relative to natural repertoires rather than the PDB. (c) Report a hierarchical or held-out error rate rather than a pooled BH.
