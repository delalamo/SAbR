IMGT vs pan-Ig numbering, domain by domain

Both schemes number on the same 1–128 V-domain frame. Pan-Ig splits the frame into 82 anchors, structural claims checked across domain types, and positional numbers filled between anchors by IMGT's CDR rule. Pick a domain type to see where its strands sit in the fold, which numbers change and where the fill rule takes over. Each type is shown through one representative structure; the statistics cover all domains of that type in the dataset.

    Rules and details that are easy to miss

    What stays the same as IMGT

    • The 1–128 V-domain frame and strand positions (A 1–15, B 16–26, C 39–46, C′ 47–55, C″ 66–74, D 75–84, E 85–96, F 97–104, G 118–128).
    • CDR fill rule and apex pairs: CDR1 32/33, CDR2 60/61, CDR3 111/112. Left insertions n.1, n.2…, right insertions m.k…m.1; the first extra residue goes right.
    • N-terminal overflow 1.1, 1.2… (IMGT C-domain style) and the conserved landmarks Cys23, Trp41, hydrophobic 89, Cys104 and J-motif 118.

    What pan-Ig changes

    • An anchor number is given only when the residue sits within 3.5 Å of that site (with matching Cβ direction) after superposition on the class consensus frame.
    • Every run of non-anchor numbers is a positional segment with one fixed apex. Loop insertions go to that apex: 8.x, 49.x, 55.1, 71.x, 82.x/83.x, 95.x, 98.x/99.x, instead of IMGT's C-domain points 45.x, 84.x/85.x, 15.x.
    • Strict policy: an anchor number a domain does not structurally occupy is left empty, never reused as filler. Neighbouring loop residues shift around it.
    • Residues past the last free number continue as 129, 130… instead of being forced onto G-strand numbers.

    Structural facts behind the differences

    • A/A′ switch. In V domains strand A crosses from the ABED sheet (A) to the other sheet (A′, positions 9–16, parallel to G). C1 domains have no A′, so their residues there are insertions 8.x.
    • C2/I-set and FN3 have C′, not D. The strand IMGT numbers 75–84 is H-bonded into the A′GFCC′ sheet, where C′ lives. D always belongs to the ABED sheet.
    • C1 C→D crossover. The residues IMGT writes as 45.x form a short strand on the back sheet at the V-domain C′ position (anchors 50–54).
    • C″ can switch sheets. In the Vα and Vδ representatives C″ pairs with D on the ABED sheet, so VH's C″ anchors (65–67) go unclaimed.

    Caveats

    • TRAC (TCR Cα) is mis-registered by pan-Ig. Its disulfide cysteines (IMGT 23/104, Cβ–Cβ 3.9 Å) superpose on Cβ/CH1's own disulfide, so IMGT is right. Pan-Ig slid strand B by two residues. A fix (a disulfide constraint plus a global register check) is proposed, not yet applied.
    • The local honesty test cannot see a whole strand shifted by two residues, because a β-strand repeats every ~6.8 Å. Shifts of two or more residues (FN3 G strand, some V-like registers) are unverified.
    • Strands shown here come from one representative structure per type; strand ends vary between structures.
    • γδ V statistics rest on 6 domains each; FN3 on 14 and 11.

    Data: 1,727 Ig-fold domains from the PDB, IMGT labels from IMGT/DomainGapAlign, pan-Ig labels from the 82-anchor scheme (see imgt_audit/PANIG.md on branch imgt-structural-audit). β-strands and sheets come from a Kabsch–Sander H-bond calculation on each representative structure.
